One or Two? How Many Queues? Very applied queue theory. There’s a well-known rule of thumb that one queue is better than two. When you’ve got people waiting to check out at the supermarket, having a single shared queue improves utilization and reduces wait times. The reason for this is pretty simple: it avoids the case where somebody is waiting in a queue while there’s a checker available to do the work. It also saves the sanity of the person standing behind a cheque writer or expired coupon negotiator. But one queue isn’t always better than two. The other day, I was up at Crystal Mountain, and needed to visit the ahem facilities. In the base lodge there, the men’s room includes a bank of urinals, and a row of stalls. As men’s rooms often do.

Since the launch of Sputnik, humanity has talked to its spacecraft through radio waves. Radio is great for communication through any weather, but it has hit a limit – traditional radio frequencies are highly regulated by bodies like the International Telecommunications Union, which limits the scalability of radio communications networks to meet the growing demand of bandwidth to and from space. The obvious solution is to switch to infrared lasers, which have an extremely high data transfer rate. However, according to a paper available in pre-print on arXiv from Dr. Slava Turyshev – best known to UT readers for his work on turning the Sun’s gravity into a giant telescope – the raw speed of laser communication isn’t the bottleneck, it’s the system’s reliability.

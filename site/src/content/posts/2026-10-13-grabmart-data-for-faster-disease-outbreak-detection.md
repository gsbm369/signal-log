Abstract Retail purchases can supplement clinical surveillance because changes in demand for some over-the-counter (OTC) products may appear before confirmed case reports. This article describes an early-stage concept for analysing aggregated GrabMart transaction data to identify unusual changes in product demand. The concept is not a deployed system, and it would require validation by public health experts, privacy review, and regulatory approval before any implementation. Introduction Public health agencies use sources such as clinical case reports, laboratory results, and hospital records to monitor disease activity. Retail data may offer an additional signal when demand changes across groups of symptom-related products. It cannot establish a diagnosis or replace clinical evidence.

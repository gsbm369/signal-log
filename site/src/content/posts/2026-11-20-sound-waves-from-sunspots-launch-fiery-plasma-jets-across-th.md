It feels like every week for the past few months we’ve been reporting on more research regarding the temperature difference between the Sun’s surface and its corona. This week is no exception, with a new paper in The Astrophysical Journal detailing the formation and tracking of around two hundred jet-like bursts of plasma from the surface known as “dynamic fibrils.” They serve as another piece of the puzzle of why the Sun’s outer atmosphere is hundreds of times hotter than its surface.

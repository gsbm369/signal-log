Some binary stars end up sharing a common envelope of gas, as the primary star draws material away from its companion. The envelope exerts drag on the stars and they can spiral toward each other. In some cases, this creates an eruption or explosion called a luminous red nova. For the first time, astronomers got to observe one of these in real time.

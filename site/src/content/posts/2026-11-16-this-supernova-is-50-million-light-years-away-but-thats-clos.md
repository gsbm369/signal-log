A new supernova has been detected in a spiral galaxy about 50 million light years away. That's close enough for detatiled follow-up observations, which have already begun. It's a type Ia supernova, meaning it's an exploding white dwarf rather than a massive star ending its life in a core-collapse detonation. Astronomers will watch as its light fades and changes over the next days, months, and years to track the changes and learn more about these standard candles. It's close enough for astrophotographers to get in on the action, too.

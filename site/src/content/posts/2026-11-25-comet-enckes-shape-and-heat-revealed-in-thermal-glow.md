Comets are time capsules of the origins of the solar system, containing materials that have existed for billions of years. While comets primarily orbit in the farthest reaches of the solar system, specifically the Kuiper Belt or Oort Cloud, some make their way closer to the Sun. When this happens, the increased heat cause cometary icy material to evaporate, producing a spectacular tail while also revealing the rocky material underneath, known as a comet’s nucleus. But how well can astronomers use telescopic observations to study the composition and temperatures of a cometary nucleus?

The short story Consider a Friday evening. A food order arrives from a mall in the city center. One driver is nearby; another is finishing a drop-off and will be available shortly; a second order from the same mall may or may not appear in the next two minutes. Dispatch the nearby driver now, or hold briefly for a batching opportunity? The decision window is short. A fulfillment marketplace makes these decisions continuously. Each one is small. Across a city, those decisions determine whether your dinner arrives hot and whether a driver’s hour is well spent. And that is one decision. There are dozens more: how far to look for a driver, when two orders are worth combining, which of three waiting trips gets the one free bike, how long to keep trying before giving up.

Apple has made a change to its development process for iOS 28, macOS 28, and corresponding software updates, according to Bloomberg 's Mark Gurman. In his Power On newsletter today , he said Apple separates development of each year's new operating systems into three major stages. Typically, he said each stage consists of four weeks of coding followed by a four-week "convergence" period where new features are integrated into the operating systems. This time around, Apple has made a change. The first stage of development for iOS 28 and the other updates apparently consisted of six weeks of coding and two weeks of feature convergence. "Instead of the normal split between two four-week efforts, Apple engineers were tasked with six weeks of coding, exploration and feature development, and then only two weeks of convergence," he said.
